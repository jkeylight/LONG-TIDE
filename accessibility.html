Accessibility

Version 1.0Reviewed 16 November 2026Target: WCAG 2.2 AA

This site was built to be read by everyone who needs it, and it tries to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA. Below is what has been done, and — more usefully — what has not.

01 · Commitment

Accessibility is not a feature bolted on at the end. For a therapy practice it matters more than usual: someone looking for help is quite likely to be in pain, exhausted, anxious or using a screen reader on a bad day. A site that is tiring to use is a site that turns people away.

02 · What has been done

  • Reduced motion is honoured throughout. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the preloader, the page wipe, the custom cursor, every scroll animation and the 3D atmosphere are all switched off, and content appears in its final state.
  • Nothing depends on JavaScript to be readable. Every animation is applied by script; if scripting fails, the page is simply already visible. This was tested with scripting disabled entirely.
  • Keyboard everywhere. The index overlay opens, closes on Escape, returns focus, and every control has a visible focus ring drawn in the accent colour against the dark background.
  • Semantic structure. One h1 per page, a single main, landmarks, lists that are lists, and headings that do not skip levels.
  • Images described. Every image carries meaningful alternative text; decorative layers are hidden from assistive technology.
  • Forms labelled. Every field has a real label, errors are tied to their field with aria-describedby, and status messages are announced through a live region.
  • No reliance on colour alone. Errors are marked with text and an outline, not only with the accent colour.
  • No time limits, no autoplaying audio, no carousels.

03 · Known limitations

Being straight about this is more useful than a badge:

  • Some small metadata text is below AA contrast. The faint letter-spaced labels used as decoration — volume numbers, marginalia, hairline captions — sit between 1.6:1 and 3.3:1 against the background. Body text and all legal text meet 4.5:1. Raising the decorative labels to 4.5:1 is possible but would visibly flatten the design, so it has not been done by default. If you find them hard to read, tell me and I will raise them.
  • The design is motion-first. Reduced motion removes the animation, but the aesthetic is a dark, high-contrast, typographic one. If reading light text on a near-black field is uncomfortable, your browser's own reader mode or a high-contrast setting will override it.
  • No audio description or captions, because the site carries no video with speech.
  • The custom cursor is decorative only. It is disabled on touch and coarse pointers, and no action depends on it.

04 · How this was tested

Automated checks of heading structure, alternative text, form labelling, horizontal overflow, focus order and console errors across all pages at desktop and mobile widths; manual keyboard-only passes; and a full run with JavaScript disabled and with reduced motion enabled. Automated tools catch a minority of real problems, so this page does not claim they caught everything.

05 · If something does not work

If any part of this site prevents you from doing what you came to do, please tell me. Email demo@example.com or telephone . Describe what happened in whatever way is easiest for you — there is no form to fill in and no wrong way to say it.

I will try to fix it, and if the site cannot be made to work for you, I will arrange another way to give you the same information.
