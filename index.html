01 — The premise

Sutherland · 1899

A final-year osteopathy student held two skull bones and noticed the seams were bevelled, overlapping — shaped like the gills of a fish. Formed, he thought, for breathing.

It took him decades to test the intuition. What emerged was a body of work built on one observation: the whole system expresses a slow, involuntary motility — an inhalation and exhalation that continues whether or not you attend to it.

02 — In the room

Almost nothing happens. That is the work.

You stay fully clothed, usually on your back. My hands rest — they do not press. The touch is light enough to feel the movement underneath it rather than create any of my own.

  1. 01ArrivalA conversation. What brought you, what you have tried, what you want to change.
  2. 02ContactHands at the feet, the sacrum or the head. Stillness, held open.
  3. 03The holistic shiftThe system settles as a whole rather than in parts.
  4. 04Still pointRhythms quieten. The fulcrum around which everything turns.
  5. 05IntegrationYou come back slowly, and we talk briefly about what you noticed.

03 — Who comes

What people bring to the table.

This is complementary care. It does not replace medical diagnosis or treatment, and it does not claim to cure any condition. If something is acute or changing quickly, see your doctor first — I will work alongside that care.

  • 01Persistent headaches, migraine and jaw tension
  • 02Neck, shoulder and low back pain that keeps returning
  • 03Nervous-system exhaustion, anxiety, difficulty sleeping
  • 04Pregnancy, birth preparation, and the weeks after
  • 05Infants: feeding difficulty, colic, unsettled sleep
  • 06Concussion and head injury, alongside medical care
  • 07Post-viral fatigue and long recovery
  • 08Pelvic pain and scar tissue after surgery
  • 09Simply wanting an hour where nothing is asked of you
