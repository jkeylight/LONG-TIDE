Medical disclaimer

Version 1.0Applies from 16 November 2026Read before booking

The short version. Craniosacral therapy is a complementary therapy. It is not a substitute for medical diagnosis, treatment or emergency care. It does not treat or cure any medical condition. Nothing on this website is medical advice, and no result is promised.

01 · If this is an emergency

Do not use this website or its contact form. If there is immediate danger to life or a medical emergency, get help now.

These are Indian national services. If you are elsewhere, use your local emergency number. Craniosacral therapy has no role in an emergency.

02 · This is not medical advice

Everything on this site is general information about one therapy, written by a practitioner of that therapy. It is not medical advice, it is not tailored to you, and it should not be used to diagnose, treat or manage any condition. Reading it does not create a practitioner–client relationship.

Always speak to a qualified doctor, nurse or other registered healthcare professional about your own circumstances. Never delay seeking medical advice because of something you read here, and never stop or alter prescribed treatment on the basis of it.

03 · No cure, no guarantees

Some people report feeling calmer, sleeping better, or moving more comfortably after sessions. Others feel very little or nothing. Reports of benefit are individual experiences; they are not predictions of what will happen for you, and they are not clinical proof.

No outcome is promised or implied. Sentences on this site such as “may help” or “some people find” are chosen deliberately. If you are looking for a guaranteed result, this is not the right treatment.

04 · How the therapy is regulated

Craniosacral therapy is not statutorily regulated in most countries, including India. That means:

  • anyone may call themselves a craniosacral therapist without breaking a licence, because no licence exists;
  • there is no legal minimum standard of training, no protected title, and no regulator to whom a complaint about a therapist can be escalated;
  • practitioners are answerable to their own certifying bodies and association codes, which vary widely.

Where a practitioner holds letters such as RCST or BCST, these come from voluntary professional bodies, not from the state. It is reasonable to ask any therapist where their training was, how long it was, and who certified them. I am happy to answer that.

05 · What a session is not

A session is not:

  • diagnosis. I do not diagnose medical conditions, and nothing said in a session is a diagnosis;
  • prescription. I do not prescribe, adjust or advise on medication;
  • spinal manipulation. This is not chiropractic or osteopathic manipulation. There is no thrust, no cracking and no high-velocity adjustment;
  • emergency or acute care. Do not come to a session instead of seeking urgent medical attention;
  • psychological therapy. It is not counselling or psychotherapy, and it is not a treatment for mental illness.

06 · Conditions that need a doctor first

Please see a doctor before booking, and tell me about it when you enquire, if you have any of the following:

  • a recent head injury, concussion or a suspected fracture;
  • aneurysm, a vascular malformation, or recent intracranial bleeding;
  • recent skull, facial or spinal surgery;
  • a shunt, or any implanted device in the head or spine;
  • uncontrolled high blood pressure;
  • a condition causing raised intracranial pressure;
  • during pregnancy, anything that a midwife or obstetrician is concerned about.

This list is not exhaustive. If you are uncertain whether this work is safe for you, treat the question as a medical one, and ask your doctor.

07 · What the research actually shows

It would be dishonest to describe the evidence as strong. The number of well-designed, independent trials is small, many are of modest quality, and results are mixed. Some reviews report no clear benefit beyond non-specific effects; others report small improvements in specific conditions. There is at present no accepted mechanism by which a “cranial rhythm” has been demonstrated.

What can be said more confidently is that sessions are gentle, and that many people find them pleasant and relaxing. If you decide to try it, go in with clear eyes: as a supportive, restful practice, not as a treatment for disease.

08 · External links

Where this site links elsewhere, including the reading list on the resources page, those books and sites are other people's work. I do not control them and I am not responsible for their content.

If anything here is unclear, or if you are unsure whether it applies to you, ask. I would far rather answer a cautious question than have someone take a risk they did not need to take.
