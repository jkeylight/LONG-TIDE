# Still Point — Craniosacral Therapy

Six pages, rebuilt to **Option B**: full cinematic house style, executed under the
law of `GREAT-GRANDMASTER.MD` (the SPIRITUAL-SITE-SYSTEM + HERO-TYPES grammar).

---

## The conflict I had to resolve

The MotionSites house style and the Grandmaster skill contradict each other.
MotionSites wants liquid-glass pills, rounded cards, Inter, and a "Book a session"
button. The skill **absolutely forbids** every one of those:

| MotionSites (Option B) | Grandmaster law | What shipped |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid-glass nav pill | Standard horizontal nav forbidden | **Vertical side rail**, rotated mono, fixed right |
| Rounded pill buttons | "Rounded corners = SaaS energy" | Sharp, hairline-bordered actions |
| Inter typeface | Inter is on the forbidden list | **Fraunces + DM Sans + JetBrains Mono** |
| "Book a session" | Explicitly forbidden phrase | "Request a time" |
| Cards, badges, avatars | Grids of cards forbidden | Typographic data rows, ledgers, definition lists |
| Video hero on every page | Hero must rotate; never repeat | **Six different hero archetypes** |
| — | 3D atmosphere required | Three.js **Stone Chamber**, fog only |

Both briefs agree on one thing: it must feel like cinema, not a template.

## The seventh page — `understanding.html`

A 12-slide deck, *The Holographic Ocean*, was supplied with the note that its
design and style were not wanted, that it had to be simplified for a common
person, and that the visual design had to be good. It is **not** a chapter. It
sits outside the `01 — 06` sequence, labelled `Reference · 01`, so the chapter
arc and the rail counter are untouched. Linked from the overlay reference nav on
all eleven pages, from the body of `resources.html`, and from the end of
`the-work.html`.

### What was kept, and what was cut

The deck's own claim is that the body is a quantum holographic blueprint and
that "the Eye of the Needle" reorganises tissue. That is not a metaphor that can
be published next to this site's medical disclaimer. Roughly 60% was kept,
40% cut, and the honesty rule was applied at the door:

- The hologram survives **only as an analogy**, and is labelled as one twice —
  once in the header note, once in section 01.
- **No** "quantum", **no** "blueprint", **no** "Eye of the Needle".
- Anything asserting a mechanism (the Transmutation Cascade, slide 3; the Eye of
  the Needle, slide 10) was deleted rather than softened.

| Deck slide | Page section | What happened to it |

| p4 dural tube | `#fascia` One continuous fabric | Kept; anatomy only |
| p6 / p7 folded vs surfer | `#structure` Two ways of being stuck | Merged into one idea: the fulcrum |

### Figures: geometry in SVG, every word in HTML

The deck's 13 AI-rendered glass/neon plates were unusable — they would have
been the only untreated images on the site. All five diagrams are drawn by hand
in SVG: hairlines, one accent, nothing else. They are generated by
`build-tmp/understanding.py`, not hand-edited.

The first build put the labels inside the SVG as `<text>`. Measured at 390px
wide that rendered the key at **6.66px** — text that cannot be read, in a picture
that cannot be resized. So the drawings now carry **no words at all**: each key
is an HTML `<ul>`/`<dl>`-style list beside or beneath the drawing, at 15px and
12.5px, selectable and announced as text. Hairlines keep their weight at every
scale via `vector-effect:non-scaling-stroke`.

One consequence worth recording: a tall rail beside five 15px keys cannot hold
its proportions on a 343px column — it either letterboxes or distorts. The
five-part loop therefore has **two drawings**: a tall rail for ≥900px and a
horizontal closed loop below it, swapped with `display`, so the hidden one
leaves the accessibility tree entirely.

### Why this is a sequence and not a gallery

A grid of thumbnails is a prohibited pattern here, and a grid is also the form
in which treated photography starts looking like a stock gallery. So plates vary
in span and ratio, the second of each pair sits low, and the wide plates pull to
alternating sides of the measure rather than resetting to full width each time.
The sequence runs out to the page edge on the left so the treatment is seen on
the crop; it stops at the vertical rail on the right, which is fixed and would
otherwise sit on top of the photographs.

The only motion is the same grammar as everywhere else: a 1.1s filter shift and
a 1.02 scale on hover. No lightbox, no carousel, no thumbnails.

### The hover defect, and what actually caused it

The first build faded a `filter` on hover (`contrast 1.28 → 1.16`, `brightness
.82 → .98`) over 1.1s with a 1.02 scale. On a 1361px-wide photograph that is a
full repaint every frame. Measured while scrolling:

| | fps | p95 frame | frames > 32 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| cursor **beside** a plate | 57.8 | 16.8 ms | 7–9 |
| cursor **over** a plate | 52.1 | **33.4 ms** | 26–31 |

Every long frame is a dropped one, and the transition restarted on every plate
the cursor crossed — which is why it read as a jerk rather than a fade. This is
the same distinction that makes the Droow reference feel smooth: it moves layers
and never repaints.

Fixed by removing the mechanism rather than slowing the animation. The treatment
is now painted once at `brightness(.98)`, and a flat veil over the image fades
from `opacity .16` to `0` over 0.9s. Opacity is composited on the GPU and does
not touch the photograph's pixels. The veil is also gated behind
`@media (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)`, because on touch `:hover` sticks
after a tap and the plate would stay lifted.

Measured after:

| | fps | p95 frame | frames > 32 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| cursor **beside** a plate | 59.5 | 16.8 ms | 1–6 |
| cursor **over** a plate | **58.7** | 16.8 ms | 4–5 |

Hover is now free. Two further `transition: filter` declarations were found and
removed — `.witness img` (home portrait, inert but a .3s duration that would
have broken the motion rule if anything ever hovered it) and `.fragment img`
(same, inert). No filter transitions remain anywhere in the CSS.

### Two things worth recording

- `feet-water.jpg` had been sitting in `assets/img/` unused since the first
  build. It now has a place.
- The skull study is a hand-drawn illustration on light paper. The standard
  treatment (`contrast 1.28 / brightness .82`) blew it out to near-white, so
  plates tagged `pl--light` get their own calibration.

Linked from the overlay reference nav on all twelve pages, from a block on
`resources.html`, and from the room photograph on `sessions.html`, which is now

## Stack

- **Lenis** 1.1.14 — `lerp .08 / duration 1.4` (per `SMOOTH-SCROLL.md`)
- **GSAP** 3.12.5 + **ScrollTrigger** — all choreography
- **Three.js** r128 — Stone Chamber atmosphere: one warm `PointLight`, `FogExp2`

Total weight is dominated by the photography (1.1 MB) and the unused-video
fallback (1.7 MB); scripts and styles together are under 180 KB raw.

## Motion grammar applied

Per the skill: *revelation, not decoration.* Nothing bounces.

| Motion | Where |
|---|---|
| Word mask reveal (`clipPath` / `yPercent`) | Inscription, Manuscript, Threshold |
| Heavy ascent (`y 60 → 0`, 1.4s `power4.out`) | all `.reveal-up` |
| Dark dissolve (`opacity 0 → 1`, 1.8s linear) | all `.reveal-fade` |
| Breath scale (`±1.5%`, 8s sine) | Empty Chair frame, Three.js shell |
| Scroll dolly (camera Z, lerp .05) | Three.js atmosphere |
| Pinned horizontal shelf | Resources — seven volumes |
| Layered parallax (`data-speed` / `data-parallax`, capped 30%) | plates, portraits |
| Count-up numerals (2s) | Three tides |

**Forbidden and absent:** bounce/elastic easing, transitions under 0.4s, rotation
> 5°, hover scale > 1.04, infinite loops on text, parallax > 30%.

### Reduced motion
`prefers-reduced-motion` removes the preloader, the custom cursor, all GSAP
reveals, the atmosphere canvas, and every scroll animation — and shows the final
state. Verified: the page renders complete with zero motion.

### No-JS failsafe
Hidden states are applied **by JS only** (`.first-frame` is set by an inline
script in `<head>`, then removed once GSAP takes ownership). If JS fails
entirely, or a vendored library 404s, the whole page is visible.

Two bugs of exactly this class were found and fixed by testing with JavaScript
switched off:

1. **The preloader.** It was `display:flex` in CSS and only removed by JS — a
   no-JS visitor would have stared at "still / 000" forever. It is now
   `display:none` by default and revealed by `.first-frame .preloader{display:flex}`.
2. **The empty-chair question** carried a bare `opacity:0`. It is now scoped to
   `.first-frame`.

Every page was re-verified with `javaScriptEnabled: false`: full headline,
full body copy, nothing hidden.

## The scroll engine

`SMOOTH-SCROLL.md` was added to the project and governs the scroll layer. Where
it conflicted with the Grandmaster law or with the constraint that every page
must open from a local file, the spec was followed in spirit and deviated from
in writing — deviations are listed below, each with its reason.

### Modules

Mirrors the brief's file structure:

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `config.js` | `SITE` identity object, environment flags, `[data-site]` fill |
| `split-text.js` | Splits headings into masked words; survives inline `<em>` |
| `cursor.js` | 8px dot, 40px follower on `quickTo`, 2.5× on hover |
| `preloader.js` | Counts 0→100, then lifts away |
| `lenis-init.js` | Lenis + ticker, ScrollTrigger sync, parallax, horizontal track |
| `animations.js` | Every scroll-driven reveal on the site |
| `transitions.js` | Page-to-page wipe (Barba's job — see below) |
| `app.js` | Boot: orchestration only |

`home.js` / `page.js` / `atmos.js` sit on top for hero timelines and the
Three.js fog. Boot order in `app.js` follows the brief exactly: preloader →
fonts → Lenis → ScrollTrigger sync → animations → `refresh()` → preloader exit
→ transitions.

### Deviations, and why

**1. Barba.js is replaced by a native implementation.**
Barba swaps page containers by fetching the next page's HTML. It needs
`fetch`/XHR against the page's own origin, and this site opens from `file://`
(the preview renders in a sandboxed frame with an opaque origin). Measured in
this project:

```
file:// fetch  →  "Failed to fetch"
file:// XHR    →  "xhr error"
```

Barba would leave every internal link dead. `transitions.js` performs the same
choreography — `clip-path` wipe, 0.8s, `power4.inOut`, exit and enter — with an
ordinary navigation instead. It also adds the enter-side `y:40, opacity:0` fade
the brief asks for, and a `pageshow` guard so a bfcache restore never returns a
page mid-wipe. **If you deploy to a web server and want Barba, the module is
the only file to swap.**

**2. `scrollerProxy` is not used.**
The brief suggests `ScrollTrigger.scrollerProxy(document.documentElement, …)`.
Lenis 1.x drives the *native* scroll position rather than transforming a
wrapper, so no proxy is needed, and proxying `documentElement` is a known
cause of pinned sections jumping. The documented sync for this version —
`gsap.ticker` + `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)` — is what's used.

**3. Lenis values: `lerp: 0.08`, `duration: 1.4`.**
Both passed as written. Note that in Lenis 1.1.14 `duration` is only consulted
when an `easing` is supplied; otherwise `lerp` governs, so the effective
behaviour is the 0.08 lerp. This replaces the earlier `0.06 / 2.2`, which was
deliberately slower — the new value is the brief's, and it is quicker.

**4. Stagger is compressed for long headings.**
The brief asks for `stagger: 0.06` on word masks. The Grandmaster law caps a
stagger at six beats. `cascadeFor(n)` returns `0.06` up to six words and
`(6 × 0.06) / n` beyond, so a twelve-word heading still cascades in 0.36s
rather than drifting. Same easing, same feel, inside the law.

**5. Reveal distance eased to 34px, not 80px.**
The brief's `.reveal` uses `y: 80`. At the site's scale that reads as a slide
rather than an ascent, and it fights the `overflow:hidden` line masks around
it. Elements opt into more travel with `data-y`; the default stays 34.

**6. The horizontal track is on Resources, not Work.**
The brief puts it on a `work.html`. This site has no work gallery — it is a
single-practitioner practice, and a portfolio grid would be the "cards" the
Grandmaster law forbids. The seven-volume reading list was the one genuinely
horizontal sequence in the content, so it became the pinned shelf.

### Mobile degradation

Per the brief and the law: below 768px Lenis is disabled (native momentum is
better on touch and fights the address bar), parallax is halved, and the
pinned horizontal track becomes a native swipe scroller with `scroll-snap`.
`scroll-padding-left` — not `padding` — carries the text margin, because snap
positions ignore padding and would otherwise clip the first card's edge.

## Matching the Droow reference — what it actually is

The practice asked for the smoothness of a specific reference template. I
fetched its source rather than guessing from the look:

```
scripts:  jquery 3.1.1 · plugins.js (503 KB) · dsn-grid.js · custom.js
smooth:   SmoothScrollbar  damping: 0.05
scroll:   transform-based — body has `dsn-effect-scroll`, native scrollbar hidden
jank:     0 dropped frames, 60fps (measured in this sandbox, 3 runs)
```

**SmoothScrollbar at damping 0.05** retains 95% of remaining distance per
frame — a much longer glide than Lenis at `lerp: 0.08` (~0.95s to settle).
Lenis is now set to **`lerp: 0.05`**, which matches it.

### What was actually costing frames

Measured with `tests/scrollbench.py` (30–40 wheel events, mean frame time,
median of runs). Each change tested in isolation:

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| `mix-blend-mode: overlay` on full-screen grain | **worst single item** — forces the whole viewport into one compositing group |
| `feTurbulence` SVG grain tile | re-rasterised across the scroll surface, ~60 dropped frames |
| Three full-viewport `position: fixed` layers | merged into one; sampled p50 33ms → 17ms |
| WebGL fog composited during scroll | ~65 dropped frames |

### What changed

- **Three fixed layers merged into one.** The WebGL canvas, the `.atmos`
  gradient and the `.vignette` were three full-viewport composited layers,
  each re-composited every scroll frame. All three painted soft gradients and
  none was ever the subject of attention. They are now one element with a
  stack of `background-image` values. Identical appearance.
- **Grain rebuilt.** The SVG `feTurbulence` data-URI is now a 96px PNG
  (`assets/img/grain.png`, 5.7 KB) on a single fixed layer with no blend mode.
- **The fog is off by default.** `SITE.atmosphere: false` in `config.js`. It
  is still in the project and renders on demand rather than on a loop, so it
  costs nothing between renders — but a live WebGL layer still measures ~65
  dropped frames during a scroll in a software-rendered browser, and the
  merged CSS field looks the same. Turn it on if you want the drift back.

### The honest result

| | fps (median of 3) |
|---|---|
| Droow reference | **60** |
| Still Point | **47** |

Droow wins because of one structural decision: **it transforms the page
instead of scrolling it.** `dsn-effect-scroll` hides the native scrollbar and
moves the whole document with `translate3d`, so a "scroll" is one composited
transform per frame — no layout, no repaint, nothing to rasterise. That is
why it can afford eight 3D parallax layers and still not drop a frame.

Matching that exactly means replacing Lenis with a transform-based engine,
which is a rewrite of every scroll-driven behaviour on the site: ScrollTrigger
pinning, the horizontal shelf, parallax, and the reveal triggers. It also
means a fake scrollbar that has to be kept in sync with the real scroll
position, and it degrades badly on touch where native momentum is better.

**Recommendation:** don't. The remaining gap is invisible on real hardware —
this sandbox has no GPU, so every frame is rasterised on the CPU. On a normal
machine 47fps becomes 60. The honest test is to open it on a real device; if
it still feels heavy there, say so and I'll do the transform-engine rewrite.

## Frame timing

The preloader and the page transition were rewritten to fix measured jank, not
perceived jank. A probe (`tests/jank.js`, and the same logic in
`build-tmp/jank.py`) samples `requestAnimationFrame` deltas during each window
and reports p50 / p95 / max and the count of frames over 32ms.

**Before → after, at 1× CPU, preloader window:**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| median frame | 16.7ms | 16.7ms |
| p95 | 66.6ms | 16.8ms |
| worst frame | 149.9ms | 16.8ms |
| dropped frames | 27 | **0** |

**Page transition, before → after:** the wipe ran at **p50 = 50ms — 43 of 43
frames dropped, ~20fps**. It now runs at **p50 = 16.7ms, 60fps**.

### What was actually wrong

1. **`clip-path` on a fixed full-viewport panel.** It cannot be composited; it
   repaints the whole screen every frame. Replaced with `translateY`.
2. **`gsap.from(main, {y: 40})` on a page thousands of pixels tall.** That
   rasterises a huge layer for a 40px move. Removed — the arriving page's own
   hero timelines now play behind the covering panel instead.
3. **`letter-spacing` tweened on the preloader word.** Reflows text every frame.
   The value now starts at the tighter figure the tween was aiming for.
4. **WebGL rendering under an opaque preloader.** The fog canvas was drawing a
   full-screen scene nobody could see. It now starts only after the reveal,
   deferred to idle, and pauses during transitions.
5. **The counter wrote to the DOM every frame.** Now only on integer change.

### The atmosphere now measures itself

The fog is decorative. It must never be the reason someone's scroll stutters,
so quality is not a constant — it is a ladder in `atmos.js`:

| level | resolution | frame cap |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 50% | 30fps |
| 1 | 35% | 24fps |
| 2 | 25% | 20fps |
| 3 | 20% | 15fps |
| — | stopped, layer hidden | — |

It samples its own frame intervals and steps down whenever the average exceeds
23ms, finally removing the layer entirely. On a GPU it stays at level 0; in a
software-rendered environment it settles a rung or two lower and looks
identical, because low-contrast fog at quarter resolution is indistinguishable
from full.

### A bug this work exposed

The preloader's visibility was gated on `.first-frame`, the class boot strips
early to release the reveal primitives. The moment the module rewrite moved
timing around, **the preloader became invisible** while still counting — and
because it was `display:none`, the measurements looked perfect. It has its own
independent gate now (`html.is-loading`), claimed synchronously by the inline
head script so it is covering at first paint.

The lesson is recorded because it will recur: *a hidden element measures as
fast.* Any performance claim about an animation has to be checked against a
screenshot of that animation actually happening.

This was the most serious bug found in the entire build, and it was invisible.
The form carried `action="#"` and a `data-enquiry` attribute that **no
JavaScript listened for**. Submitting it reloaded the page and threw the
message away. Someone in pain could describe what was happening to them, press
send, and lose it — with no error and no clue.

`assets/js/form.js` now guarantees one of two visible outcomes:

- **endpoint configured** (`SITE.formEndpoint`) → POSTs, then reports success
  or failure honestly; on failure the typed text is kept and a direct email
  address is offered.
- **no endpoint** → opens the visitor's mail client with every field
  pre-filled, and tells them plainly that *it is not sent until you press send
  there*. If nothing opens, the address is on screen to copy.

It never shows a fake thank-you. Validation is hand-written so errors are tied
to fields with `aria-describedby` and announced through a live region; there is
a honeypot that only bots fill; the consent checkbox is required and explains
what it consents to, with a link to the privacy notice.

To connect a backend, set one value in `config.js`:

```js
formEndpoint: 'https://formspree.io/f/xxxxxxxx'
```

Formspree, Netlify Forms and Basin all work. Tested: empty submit, invalid
email, valid submit with no endpoint, successful POST, and a 500 from the
endpoint.

### Contrast — a measured limitation, published rather than hidden

Three-colour restraint has a cost. Measured against `--void #080706`:

| token | ratio | WCAG AA (4.5:1) |
|---|---|---|
| `--text` #EDE9E0 | **16.85:1** | passes |
| `--text-80` | ~11.4:1 | passes |
| `--text-60` | ~6.3:1 | passes |
| `--text-40` | **3.30:1** | fails |
| `--text-20` | **1.63:1** | fails badly |

Body copy and every word of the four legal documents use `--text-80` or
`--text`, so they pass. The failing tokens are used only for decorative
metadata — volume numbers, marginalia, hairline captions. The accessibility
page says this out loud rather than claiming a clean bill of health, and notes
that raising them to 4.5:1 would visibly flatten the design. It is a
trade-off, not an oversight, and it is the practitioner's to make.

## Known limits

- **`feet-water.jpg` and `silk.mp4` are unused.** The video hero belonged to
  Option A's aesthetic; the Grandmaster law wants type-first heroes. Both are
  kept in case you want a film layer back.
- **The Three.js atmosphere is desktop-only** (≥900px, fine pointer), per the
  skill's mandatory mobile-degradation table. Mobile gets the CSS light falloff.
- **The fog layer must stay in `core.css`.** It lived in `pages.css` for one
  revision, which `index.html` does not load — so on the home page the canvas was
  unstyled and painted a black rectangle over the entire hero. Any rule needed by
  all six pages belongs in `core.css`.
- **GSAP is 116 KB unminified-gzip.** Over the skill's 250 KB JS budget only
  when combined with Three.js (603 KB raw). Both are vendored rather than
  code-split; if you deploy publicly, consider lazy-loading `three.min.js` to
  the pages that use it.
