An hour that asks
nothing of you.

Sessions are sixty minutes, fully clothed, and almost entirely silent. Below is everything you would want to know before a first appointment — including the parts usually left vague.

i — iv Begin

i — The sequence

What happens, in order.

Nothing is done to you, and nothing is expected of you. The shape below is the ordinary one.

    Arrival

    Ten minutes or so. What brings you here, what you have already tried, and what would tell you the session had been worthwhile. For a first appointment I also ask about birth, accidents and surgeries — the body keeps that history.

    Settling

    You lie on your back on a heated table, with a blanket. No undressing beyond removing shoes and glasses. My hands rest lightly — often under the head or at the feet — while I listen for the quality of the motion underneath.

    Following

    Most of the session is this. The system may become more symmetrical, or slow down, or pull into a still point. I follow rather than direct. Some people talk; most do not.

    Coming back

    You stay lying down until you want to move. Then we sit and talk briefly about what you noticed. You leave with water and no instructions beyond taking the rest of the day a little more gently.

iv — Questions

Before your first appointment.

Will I feel anything?

Most people feel a settling — warmth, heaviness, the sense that the room has become quieter than it is. Some feel nothing at all and find that afterwards they slept better for a week. Feeling more is not a measure of getting more.

Do you press or manipulate anything?

No. The contact is light — a few grams of weight, in most holds. Nothing is cracked, forced or adjusted. If you have had sports massage or chiropractic care, this will feel almost alarmingly gentle.

How many sessions will I need?

For an acute, recent problem, two or three sessions a fortnight apart is a common pattern. For long-standing patterns, expect to work over months rather than weeks. I review at every third session whether continuing is useful, and I will say so if I think it is not.

Is it safe during pregnancy?

Yes, and it is one of the most common reasons people come. Positions are adapted as pregnancy progresses. With any high-risk pregnancy, check with your midwife or obstetrician first.

Can you treat my baby?

I work with infants from a few weeks old, usually for feeding difficulty, colic, unsettled sleep or a difficult birth. A parent stays in the room throughout. Infant sessions are 45 minutes and mostly consist of waiting.

Will it conflict with medication?

No. Craniosacral therapy does not interact with medication and is commonly used alongside physiotherapy, psychotherapy and medical care.

What if something feels wrong afterwards?

Message me. Mild reactions — tiredness, a headache, feeling emotional, or a temporary increase in symptoms — are common in the 24–48 hours after a session and usually settle. Anything sharp, sudden or worrying should go to your doctor first.

What should I wear?

Whatever you would wear to a friend’s house. You keep everything on except shoes and glasses. Soft waistbands are kinder than belts.
