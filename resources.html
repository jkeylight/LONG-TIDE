i — Glossary

Ten terms worth knowing.

Breath of Life
Sutherland’s term for the organising principle behind the body’s involuntary motion. Used less as anatomy than as the source from which the rhythms arise.
Primary respiration
The body’s involuntary breathing motility, distinct from lung breathing. It continues regardless of attention.
Cranial rhythmic impulse
The most superficial of the three tides, felt at roughly eight to twelve cycles per minute.
The mid-tide
A slower rhythm of about two and a half cycles per minute, described as carrying the available potency that organises the body.
The long tide
The most formative rhythm, at roughly one hundred seconds per cycle. Described as boundless: it does not appear to stop at the skin.
Still point
A pause in the rhythm. Not an absence of motion but the fulcrum around which motion organises.
Dynamic stillness
The ground from which the tides arise — reached by quietening enough to notice it.
Holistic shift
The moment the physiology settles as a whole rather than in parts, expressing wholeness rather than fragmentation.
Reciprocal tension
The balanced, continuous tension of the membranes inside the head, which lets motion distribute through the whole system.
Potency
The available vitality carried in the fluids — not a force the practitioner adds, but one they make room for.

ii — Volumes

If you want to understand this properly, read these.

In the order I would give them to a curious client. The first two are for anyone; the rest are practitioner texts.

01

Wisdom in the Body

Michael Kern

The clearest single introduction to the cranial concept and to biodynamic practice. If you read one book, read this one.

Start here
02

Every Body Tells a Story

Liz Kalinowska & Daška Hatton

A craniosacral journey told through practitioners’ and patients’ accounts. The best answer to “what does it feel like?”

For clients
03

The Breath of Life

Cherionna Menzam-Sills

An introduction to craniosacral biodynamics that stays close to the felt experience. Good on the tides.

Biodynamics
04

The foundational practitioner text of the biodynamic approach. Dense, systematic, and the source of much of this vocabulary.
